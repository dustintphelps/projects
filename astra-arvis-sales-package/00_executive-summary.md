## The Bottom Line

**2026 is the most financially favorable year in a decade to purchase capital imaging and navigation equipment.** Two independent forces converged simultaneously — and neither existed 18 months ago.

**Force 1 — Tax Law:** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), signed July 4, 2025 (Public Law 119-21), permanently restored 100% first-year bonus depreciation. This is not temporary. There is no phasedown, no sunset, no dollar cap. A $300,000 Astra Arvis system purchased and placed in service today generates a **$300,000 federal tax deduction in the current year** — returning $111,000 to a surgeon-owner at the 37% bracket, or $63,000 to a for-profit hospital at the 21% corporate rate. Under prior law, 2026 would have been a 20% bonus depreciation year — a $60,000 deduction on that same $300,000 purchase, with $240,000 deducted slowly over five more years. The OBBBA delivered an **$87,000–$153,000 improvement in Year 1 cash position** on that single purchase depending on tax bracket.

**Force 2 — CMS Coverage:** The CY 2026 OPPS/ASC Final Rule added **560 surgical procedures and 35 ancillary services** to the ASC Covered Procedures List and removed **285 musculoskeletal procedures from the Inpatient-Only list** — meaning complex joint revisions and multilevel spine fusions that were exclusively billed as hospital admissions 12 months ago are now performable and billable in outpatient and ASC settings. These are the highest-facility-fee procedures in orthopedics. Performing them safely in an ambulatory environment requires navigation and imaging. Facilities without that capability are excluded from the highest-value tier of the newly expanded market.

The argument for purchasing Arvis in 2026 is not manufactured urgency. It is the intersection of two policy changes that both independently favor action now.

## The Seven Numbers That Define This Conversation

**1. $126,000** — Real after-tax cost of a $200,000 Astra Arvis system for a surgeon-owner at the 37% federal bracket using 100% bonus depreciation. The remaining $74,000 is covered by the federal tax system in Year 1. This is not a rebate or an incentive — it is the permanent law of the land.

**2. ~7 months** — Estimated EBITDA payback period for a navigation system enabling 50 additional total joint cases per year. At orthopedic ASC average contribution margins ($375,000 incremental EBITDA on 50 TJA cases), a $200,000 system after tax ($126,000 net cost) pays for itself before the end of its first full year of operation.

**3. $3.375M** — Estimated increase in ASC enterprise value from $375,000 in incremental annual EBITDA at a 9x EBITDA multiple (conservative for orthopedic ASCs; the observed range is 7–10x, with platform ASCs reaching 11–17x). A surgeon holding 25% equity captures approximately $843,000 in equity value appreciation — on a $31,500 net after-tax capital contribution (25% share of $200K after 37% tax savings).

**4. 9.0% vs. 31.8%** — Rate of mechanical axis malalignment outliers (>3°) in computer-assisted navigation TKA versus conventional TKA in the peer-reviewed meta-analysis literature. This is a greater than 70% relative reduction in the primary surgical quality metric for total knee arthroplasty — and it is the clinical foundation for the outcomes-based commercial payer contracting conversation.

**5. $2.3 billion** — Annual Medicare savings when procedures are performed in ASCs rather than HOPDs, per ASCA. UnitedHealth Group projects $70 billion in Medicare savings over the next decade from the TJA site-of-service shift alone. Commercial payers are responding accordingly: they are actively steering joint replacement volume to ASC settings through differential cost-sharing, narrow-network construction, and prior authorization policies. Navigation-capable ASCs are the beneficiaries of this trend; those without the technology cannot safely perform the procedure categories payers are steering toward them.

**6. 20.4%** — Share of total knee arthroplasties performed with robotic or navigation assistance at the end of 2023 (AJRR data), up from less than 5% in 2017. By 2028, more than half of total joints are projected to be performed in outpatient settings. The technology adoption curve in orthopedic navigation has gone from early adopter to early majority. Facilities that have not yet adopted are approaching the point where the question shifts from "should we?" to "why haven't we?"

**7. June 30, 2026** — Hard construction-start deadline for the Section 179D energy-efficient commercial building deduction (OBBBA terminated it for construction beginning after this date). For first-time ASC developers building a facility with qualifying HVAC, lighting, or building envelope work, a 10,000 sq. ft. center breaking ground before this date captures $25,000–$50,000 in additional federal tax deductions. The clock is running on this one.

## Our Position: Act in 2026

We believe the financial case for purchasing Astra Arvis in 2026 is stronger than it will be in any subsequent year in the near term — not because of artificial scarcity or expiring promotions, but because of the specific convergence of factors outlined above.

**The tax benefit is permanent now, but the present value erodes with deferral.** A $74,000 tax deduction taken in 2027 instead of 2026 is worth approximately $5,900 less in present value at an 8% discount rate — and that is before accounting for the revenue foregone during the deferral period. Every month a navigation system is not installed is a month the highest-acuity, highest-revenue orthopedic cases are performed elsewhere.

**The procedure eligibility window is wide open.** CMS's 2026 rule changes created the broadest-ever ASC billing scope for complex orthopedic surgery. Revision total hip, revision total knee, and complex spine fusions are newly accessible. The facilities that equip for these procedures in 2026 establish clinical programs, surgeon relationships, and payer contracting positions that will be durable advantages. Those that wait give their competitors 12–24 months to build the same advantages first.

**Surgeon recruitment depends on technology availability, and the supply of trained surgeons is constrained.** The U.S. faces a projected shortage of 5,080 orthopedic surgeons by 2025. In a supply-constrained market, technology-equipped facilities recruit from a larger pool; those without navigation recruit from what remains after competitors have hired. First-mover advantage in surgeon alignment compounds annually.

The financial case does not require optimistic assumptions. It holds at conservative case volume, conservative revenue per case, and conservative EBITDA multiples. Our recommendation is unambiguous: purchase in 2026, own the equipment (do not lease), and do so before the fiscal year closes.
