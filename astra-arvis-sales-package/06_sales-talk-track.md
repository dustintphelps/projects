# Section 6: Sales Rep Talk Track & Objection Handling

> This guide is for internal sales agent use only. All financial figures are based on publicly available 2026 data for conversational context — not financial advice. Always direct customers to their own CPA or financial advisor for specific guidance. The disclaimer at the end of every conversation matters — use it.

---

## Before You Walk In: Identify Your Buyer

The biggest mistake a sales rep makes in this conversation is leading with the wrong primary argument. Each buyer type has a different financial worldview. Mismatching the opening costs credibility and often the deal.

| Buyer Type | Their Primary Language | Wrong Opening | Right Opening |
|---|---|---|---|
| For-profit hospital CFO/VP | Capital efficiency, tax impact, margin | Clinical outcomes | "The OBBBA gave you $63K back on a $300K purchase" |
| Non-profit hospital administrator/CMO | Volume, quality, competitive position | Tax savings (they don't benefit) | "285 procedures came off the IPO list. Which ones can your program bill for today?" |
| Established ASC surgeon-partner | After-tax cash, equity value, distributions | Hospital competition | "The federal government covers 37 cents of every dollar you spend on this" |
| First-time ASC developer | Business model fundamentals, risk management | Advanced tax strategy | Break-even math + equipment timing rationale |

## Talk Track 1: For-Profit Hospital CFO or VP Finance

**Setting:** Budget meeting, capital committee presentation, or finance-focused follow-up after a clinical demo.

**Opening:**
> "Before we get into system specifics, I want to spend two minutes on the capital side — because the timing conversation has changed materially in the last 12 months. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed last July, permanently restored 100% first-year bonus depreciation on equipment like this. That's not a temporary provision — it's permanent law. On a $300,000 purchase at your corporate rate, that's $63,000 in immediate tax savings — cash that stays on your balance sheet this fiscal year. Under what the law would have been without OBBBA, 2026 was a 20% bonus depreciation year — you would have recovered $12,600 in Year 1 and the rest over five more years. The law gave you back $50,400 in Year 1 value on that single purchase."

**The volume retention pivot:**
> "The other piece — and this is more directly in your orthopedic team's territory — is what CMS did in January 2026. They removed 285 musculoskeletal procedures from the Inpatient-Only list. Revision total hip, revision total knee, complex multilevel fusions — now billable in outpatient settings. These are your highest-facility-fee cases. The facilities that are going to capture that outpatient volume are the ones with navigation capability in their outpatient program.

> Your HOPD earns approximately $14,000 per TKA on Medicare — the ASC rate for the same case is $9,284. That's a $4,700 spread per case. You retain that spread as long as your surgeons perform at your facility. A navigation-equipped ASC or competing hospital that recruits one of your high-volume joint surgeons takes that $4,700 per case permanently. At 200 TKAs per year per surgeon, that's $940,000 in annual facility fee revenue walking out the door with one recruitment."

**The GPO close:**
> "Before you finalize any capital commitment, confirm whether you have a Vizient or Premier group purchase contract or active group buy on navigation equipment. The documented savings on capital equipment group purchases run 10–27% off list. On a $300,000 system, a 15% GPO discount is $45,000 that changes your payback calculation. We can work within any GPO pricing structure. Can we schedule 30 minutes with your supply chain team to review your contract access?"

## Talk Track 2: Non-Profit Hospital Administrator or CMO

**Setting:** Strategic planning discussion, orthopedic service line review, or competitive positioning meeting.

**Opening — do NOT lead with tax:**
> "I want to be direct about something upfront: the tax depreciation story that dominates conversations with for-profit systems doesn't apply to you, and I'm not going to pretend it does. The financial case for your system is built entirely on what CMS changed in January 2026 and what it means for your orthopedic program's competitive position over the next three years.

> Two hundred and eighty-five musculoskeletal procedures came off the Inpatient-Only list. CMS created a new Level 7 Musculoskeletal APC to accommodate complex joint revisions and multilevel fusions that were exclusively billable as hospital admissions 12 months ago. Those cases are now available in outpatient settings — and the facilities that perform them outpatient need navigation capability to do it safely."

**The migration argument:**
> "Sg2 projects that 51% of total joints will be performed outpatient by 2026. That's not a prediction — it's already happening. ASC joint replacement volume grew 293% from 2019 to 2023. The question your orthopedic leadership needs to answer is: when your surgeons' patients are asking for outpatient joint replacement, are they getting it here or somewhere else?

> Your on-campus HOPD earns approximately $14,005 per TKA on Medicare. An ASC earns $9,284. You have a $4,700 per-case advantage that exists as long as you're performing those cases in your facility. If a navigation-equipped ASC pulls your high-volume total joint surgeon — and surgeons trained in the last five years increasingly prefer facilities with navigation — that $4,700 spread migrates with them."

**The quality argument:**
> "Computer-assisted navigation reduces implant malalignment outliers from 31.8% to 9% in TKA — that's a 71% relative reduction in the primary quality metric for joint replacement. In your inpatient DRG environment, every avoided revision is margin improvement that doesn't come with a second reimbursement. Your DRG 470 payment is approximately $14,200–$15,000. A revision case doesn't generate a new DRG payment — it generates additional cost. Navigation turns revision avoidance into direct operating margin."

**For spine programs — the billing differentiation:**
> "One thing specific to your spine program: spine navigation CPTs 61781–61783 are separately billable under OPPS. Unlike extremity navigation codes, which are bundled under comprehensive APCs, spine navigation generates an incremental billing item on top of the spine fusion facility fee. That's a direct reimbursement argument that doesn't exist for total joint navigation — and it's worth running through your spine billing team."

## Talk Track 3: Established Surgeon-Partner in an Existing ASC

**Setting:** Any — office, OR lounge, lunch. This is a relationship conversation first.

**Opening:**
> "I want to show you something quickly before we talk about the system. On a $200,000 purchase at your tax bracket — let's use 37% — the real after-tax cost is $126,000. The other $74,000 is a federal tax deduction taken in the same year you install it. That's not a promotion or a rebate — it's permanent law now. Bonus depreciation was restored to 100% last July under the Big Beautiful Bill, permanently. No phase-down, no sunset.

> That deduction flows through your K-1 to your personal return in proportion to your ownership stake. If you own 25% of the ASC, you get $50,000 of that deduction — worth $18,500 in personal tax savings. Your capital contribution for your 25% share was $50,000; your after-tax cost is $31,500. Before the system generates a single case."

**The revenue and equity angle:**
> "Now let's talk about what the system actually does for your center. If it enables 50 additional high-acuity cases per year — total joints, revisions, complex cases — at a blended $10,000 net revenue and $4,500 contribution margin, that's $225,000 in incremental EBITDA. Your 25% distributable share is $56,250 per year in additional distributions.

> But the number that I think is most under-appreciated is what that EBITDA does to your equity value. Orthopedic ASCs are trading at 7–10x EBITDA right now. $225,000 in incremental EBITDA at 9x is $2,025,000 in enterprise value added. Your 25% of that is $506,000 in equity value. On a $31,500 after-tax investment."

**The 2026 procedure expansion angle:**
> "And this is specific to 2026 — revision total hip and revision total knee are now on the ASC covered procedure list for the first time. Those are your highest-facility-fee cases: $9,500–$12,000 Medicare ASC, $15,000–$25,000 commercial. Navigation isn't optional for revision cases in an ASC setting — it's what makes them possible. If your center isn't equipped for revision arthroplasty, you're excluded from a procedure category that just became newly eligible. That's revenue your center is currently leaving on the table by not being equipped for it."

**Handling 'we'll think about it for next year's budget':**
> "Here's what a one-year delay actually costs. The depreciation benefit is permanent — you don't lose the deduction by waiting. But the revenue is gone forever. If your center does 4 additional TJAs per month at $10,000 blended, that's $480,000 in procedure revenue over the 12 months you spent deliberating. At your margin rate, that's roughly $180,000 in EBITDA. At 9x, that's $1.62 million in enterprise value you didn't build. The present-value cost of deferring the tax deduction itself is about $5,900 at 8% discount rate. The real cost of waiting is the foregone operating performance, not the tax timing."

## Talk Track 4: First-Time ASC Developer

**Setting:** Initial education meeting, feasibility discussion, or physician investor group presentation.

**Opening:**
> "The financial model for a de novo orthopedic ASC is more compelling right now than it has been at any point in the last 10 years. The average orthopedic ASC generates $6,419 in net revenue per case — highest of any specialty. CMS just added 560 new billable procedures to the ASC covered list, including revision joints that were inpatient-only 12 months ago. A two-OR center breaks even at approximately 65–80 cases per month, and a committed orthopedic group running both ORs at standard utilization generates 1,500–1,700 cases per year — well above that break-even line.

> The challenge isn't the steady-state economics. The challenge is the 12–18 month ramp from opening to full utilization, and the 4–6 months before payer contracting is complete and cash is consistently flowing. That's where most first-time projects get into trouble — not because the business model doesn't work, but because they underestimated the working capital requirement. Budget 6–8 months of operating costs as liquid reserves. This is non-negotiable."

**The equipment timing argument:**
> "One of the most important capital decisions you'll make is whether to fully equip the center at opening. My recommendation is unambiguous: do it. Here's why.

> Under the current tax law — permanent 100% bonus depreciation — a $300,000 Arvis system placed in service in your first operating year generates a $300,000 entity-level deduction that flows through to every physician-owner's personal return. Across a 4-surgeon group at the 37% bracket, that's $111,000 in total tax savings in the year when your startup costs are highest and you need every cash offset available.

> The revenue argument is even simpler: orthopedic surgery requires imaging and navigation to perform your highest-value procedures. You cannot safely perform total joints, revision arthroplasty, or complex spine in an ambulatory setting without this technology. Every month you operate without it, those cases — your $9,000–$12,000 per-case revenue generators — go somewhere else. The delay pays for nothing except giving your competitors more time with your patients."

**On financing:**
> "For equipment specifically — finance with a loan, not an operating lease. A true lease means the lender owns the equipment, not you. You cannot claim bonus depreciation on equipment you don't own. At the 37% bracket, that's $111,000 in Year 1 tax savings you forfeit by leasing instead of owning. Healthcare equipment lenders are doing 7–11% for qualified physician groups. The after-tax cost of a 9% loan on $300,000 is about $17,000 annually after the deduction. Compare that to $111,000 in Year 1 tax savings from ownership. The math strongly favors buying."

## The Four Objections Every Rep Encounters

### Objection 1: "It's not in this year's capital budget."

**For hospitals:**
> "What is your authorization threshold for unbudgeted capital items? At most systems, a $100–$500K revenue-generating purchase falls within CFO or VP-level authorization — you don't need a full board cycle. If you can frame this as a revenue retention investment — which it is, at $4,700 per case in HOPD vs. ASC differential — it often qualifies for mid-year approval. Let me put together a one-page retention analysis for your committee. What's your total annual TKA volume?"

**For ASC surgeon-owners:**
> "The bonus depreciation benefit doesn't expire — it's permanent law now. But the tax deduction is a 2026 deduction only if the equipment is placed in service before December 31st. January 2nd is a different tax year. And every month of deferral is procedure revenue your competitors capture. Is there a specific budget event that determines the timeline, or is this a cash flow question?"

### Objection 2: "We need to see better ROI data."

> "The ROI question has one variable that dominates everything else: how many cases per month does your committed surgeon group bring to this system? Let me give you specific numbers.

> Navigation reduces malalignment outliers from 31.8% to 9% — that's the peer-reviewed meta-analysis result. For revision avoidance value in a bundled payment environment: each avoided revision at roughly $20,000 in hospital cost represents real margin improvement. For volume-driven ROI: 4 additional TJA cases per month at $10,000 blended revenue generates $480,000 annually. A $200,000 system with a $126,000 after-tax cost — factoring in bonus depreciation — pays back in under 4 months of incremental revenue from just 4 cases per month.

> Can we spend 30 minutes building a utilization model based on your specific surgeon commitments and payer mix? I'll bring a clinical specialist who can walk through the workflow and the case attribution analysis."

### Objection 3: "We're evaluating [Competitor X]."

> "That's a comparison worth doing thoroughly. What I'd ask you to evaluate side by side is: total cost of ownership including service contract terms and upgrade cycle obligations, the specific procedure categories each system enables and how they align with your case mix, and OR setup time at steady state after the learning curve. Navigation learning curves vary — the literature shows 9–20 cases to time neutrality depending on the system. That's a 1–2 month window of operational adjustment, not a permanent overhead.

> I'd like to bring our clinical specialist in to do a workflow comparison specific to your OR setup and case types. Peer-to-peer surgeon conversations with current users are also available — in my experience, that reference call is more useful than any demo we can run. Would that be helpful?"

### Objection 4: "We want to wait and see how the reimbursement landscape develops."

> "I want to address the reimbursement question directly, because there's a common misconception. CMS does not pay a facility premium for navigation-assisted procedures in the joint replacement setting — extremity navigation codes are bundled under comprehensive APCs. No one should be selling you on a reimbursement premium for joint navigation that doesn't exist.

> The financial case is not a billing premium argument. It is a volume argument: navigation capability is the prerequisite for performing the procedure categories that carry the highest existing facility fees — total joints, revisions, complex cases. It is a quality argument: better alignment accuracy supports favorable outcomes data that drives commercial payer contracting leverage, COE designation, and patient demand. And it is a competitive positioning argument: 20% of TKAs now use robotic/navigation assistance — up from less than 5% in 2017. The facilities without navigation are not 'waiting and seeing.' They are watching the market pass them by."

## Closing Framework: Always End With a Specific Next Step

Never leave a conversation without a committed next action. Options in order of deal-velocity value:

1. **30-minute financial modeling session** with the finance team and your clinical specialist — "I'll build a custom utilization analysis with your case volume and payer mix"
2. **Site visit to a reference ASC** using Arvis — surgeon-to-surgeon conversations close more than any presentation
3. **GPO quote request** through their Vizient/Premier membership — "Let me get you the contracted pricing so your committee has real numbers"
4. **Clinical specialist workflow review** — "Bring your OR director, I'll bring our clinical team, let's spend an hour on the logistics"
