## What Changed — And Why It Matters Now

The **One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA)**, signed into law on **July 4, 2025 (Public Law 119-21)**, permanently reversed the tax depreciation phasedown that had been eroding the value of capital equipment purchases since 2023. The result: 2026 is the first full calendar year in which every dollar spent on qualifying equipment like Astra Arvis can be recovered **entirely in the year of purchase** — with no expiration date on that benefit.

---

## The Three Provisions That Drive the Entire Analysis

### 1. Bonus Depreciation — 100%, Permanent

Under the prior schedule, bonus depreciation was:
- 2023: 80% → 2024: 60% → 2025: 40% → 2026 (old law): 20% → 2027: 0%

**OBBBA reversed this entirely.** For any equipment **acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025**, bonus depreciation is **100% with no sunset**.

**What this means in dollars:** A $300,000 Astra Arvis system purchased and installed in 2026 generates a $300,000 federal tax deduction in the current tax year. At the 37% individual rate (surgeon-owner), that is **$111,000 returned to the owner in the same year**. At the 21% corporate rate (for-profit hospital), it is **$63,000**.

Unlike Section 179, bonus depreciation has **no income limitation** — it can create a net operating loss that carries forward. It requires no income to absorb it.

**Critical date:** The acquisition date (contract signed, binding commitment) AND the placed-in-service date must both be after January 19, 2025. Equipment purchased and delivered today qualifies without question.

---

### 2. Section 179 — Limit Doubled to $2,560,000

Section 179 allows businesses to immediately expense qualifying equipment up to the annual limit, subject to available taxable income.

| | Before OBBBA | 2026 (Post-OBBBA) |
|---|---|---|
| Deduction limit | ~$1.22M | **$2,560,000** |
| Phase-out begins | ~$2.62M | $4,090,000 |
| Income limitation | Yes | Yes |

For most ASC and hospital equipment budgets well below $4M in annual purchases, the full deduction is available. The income limitation means Section 179 cannot create a loss — but for facilities with taxable income, it is the cleanest first-year write-off available.

**Practical use:** Section 179 and bonus depreciation together allow a facility purchasing multiple pieces of equipment in a single year to expense the entire purchase price with maximum flexibility — Section 179 first (up to taxable income), bonus depreciation on any remainder.

---

### 3. QBI Deduction (Section 199A) — Permanent at 23%

The 20% pass-through deduction (now **raised to 23%** under OBBBA for tax years beginning after December 31, 2025) has been made permanent with no sunset.

**The critical limitation for physicians:** Healthcare is classified as a Specified Service Trade or Business (SSTB). The QBI deduction phases out for physician-owners with taxable income above approximately $395,000 (single) or $545,000 (married filing jointly) for 2026 and is eliminated entirely above those thresholds.

**The planning lever:** A large Section 179 or bonus depreciation deduction can pull a surgeon's taxable income *below* the phase-out threshold, potentially recovering the full 23% QBI deduction on remaining business income. A surgeon sitting at $500,000 of taxable income who takes a $150,000 equipment deduction may recover the full QBI deduction — worth an additional $14,000–$18,000 in tax savings on top of the depreciation benefit itself.

For high-income surgeons already well above the threshold: the QBI benefit is unavailable regardless, but bonus depreciation and Section 179 still deliver the same powerful first-year write-off.

## Additional OBBBA Provisions With Direct Impact

### Estate & Gift Tax — $15M Individual Exemption (Effective January 1, 2026)

The estate and gift tax exemption was raised from approximately $13.99M to **$15M per individual ($30M per married couple)** — permanently, with no sunset. This is directly relevant to:

- Surgeons evaluating ASC ownership structures and long-term succession planning
- Physician groups considering buy-sell agreements and partner buyouts
- ASC ownership interests valued in the $5M–$20M range that previously carried estate tax exposure

For a surgeon-owner holding a 25% stake in a $20M-valued ASC, the full $5M ownership interest now passes estate-tax-free below the $15M individual threshold.

### Section 163(j) EBITDA Restoration — Immediate Benefit for Leveraged Buyers

For facilities financing their Arvis purchase with debt, the OBBBA restored the interest deduction limit to an **EBITDA-based calculation** (rather than EBIT). This directly increases the amount of interest expense deductible in any given year for capital-intensive, debt-financed ASC structures — making leveraged equipment acquisitions more tax-efficient.

### Section 179D — Construction Start Deadline June 30, 2026

For ASCs constructing or renovating facilities with qualifying HVAC, lighting, or building envelope improvements: the OBBBA **terminates the Section 179D energy-efficiency deduction for construction beginning after June 30, 2026**. A 10,000 sq. ft. ASC with qualifying improvements can generate a $25,000–$50,000 deduction. The clock is running.

## The After-Tax Cost Matrix

*Based on a $300,000 Astra Arvis system, full first-year expensing elected, 2026 tax year:*

| Buyer Type | Tax Rate | Year 1 Tax Savings | Net After-Tax Cost | Savings vs. Old 20% Bonus Dep. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Surgeon-owner (individual, 37%) | 37% | **$111,000** | **$189,000** | +$48,300 more than old law |
| For-profit hospital (C-corp, 21%) | 21% | **$63,000** | **$237,000** | +$27,300 more than old law |
| Non-profit hospital | 0% (tax-exempt) | **$0** | $300,000 | No change |

*Note: State tax savings are additive but vary by state. State conformity to OBBBA bonus depreciation varies — several states (including California) do not conform and require separate state-level depreciation. Verify with a CPA.*

## Key Caveats for Sales Conversations

- **No retroactivity:** Equipment purchased and placed in service before January 20, 2025 does not qualify for 100% bonus depreciation. Do not suggest clients can amend prior-year returns.
- **State conformity varies:** California, New York, New Jersey, and several other states do not conform to federal bonus depreciation. State-level tax savings may differ significantly from federal.
- **SSTB phase-out is real:** High-income surgeons above ~$545,000 MFJ taxable income receive zero QBI benefit. Do not overstate this provision for high earners.
- **Non-profit hospitals receive no direct tax benefit:** Their financial case is built entirely on operational ROI, volume retention, and reimbursement — covered in Section 2.
- **Consult a CPA:** All specific figures should be verified with the client's tax advisor before use in any financial decision.
