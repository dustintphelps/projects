## What Changed — The Full Picture

The **One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA)**, signed July 4, 2025 (Public Law 119-21), passed the Senate 51–50 with Vice President Vance casting the tiebreaking vote. It permanently extended and expanded most Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA) provisions that were scheduled to expire December 31, 2025 — and made several material improvements that directly benefit physician-owned businesses and healthcare facilities purchasing capital equipment.

The single most important provision for equipment acquisition: **100% bonus depreciation is permanent, with no sunset, no phasedown, and no dollar cap.**

---

## Provision 1: Bonus Depreciation — 100% Permanent

### The Before and After

The TCJA established a phasedown schedule that was scheduled to eliminate bonus depreciation entirely by 2027:

| Year | Prior TCJA Schedule | OBBBA Result |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 80% | 80% (no change — already passed) |
| 2024 | 60% | 60% (no change — already passed) |
| 2025 | 40% | **100%** |
| 2026 | 20% | **100%** |
| 2027+ | 0% | **100% (permanent)** |

### The Critical Date: January 19, 2025

The 100% rate applies only to property **both acquired AND placed in service after January 19, 2025**.

- Equipment acquired pursuant to a written binding contract **on or before January 19, 2025** — even if delivered later — is subject to the old phasedown (40% in 2025, 20% in 2026)
- Equipment where the purchase contract was signed **after January 19, 2025** and placed in service the same year: **100%**
- Any new purchase made today qualifies without question

*Practical note: If a customer is reviewing a contract from early 2025 that straddles this date, help them verify the contract execution date — it determines which depreciation rate applies.*

### How Bonus Depreciation Works in Practice

For a $300,000 Arvis system placed in service in 2026:

| | 100% Bonus Dep. (Current Law) | 20% Bonus Dep. (Old 2026 Law) | No Acceleration (5-Yr MACRS) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 deduction | $300,000 | $60,000 | $60,000 |
| Year 2 | $0 | $96,000 | $96,000 |
| Year 3 | $0 | $57,600 | $57,600 |
| Years 4–6 | $0 | Remainder | Remainder |
| **Year 1 tax savings (37%)** | **$111,000** | **$22,200** | **$22,200** |
| **NPV of all-year savings (8% disc.)** | **$111,000** | **$85,600** | **$74,000** |

**The OBBBA restored $88,800 in Year 1 value** on a $300,000 purchase at the 37% bracket versus what 2026 law would have been without it. That is not a planning opportunity — that is a law that has already been signed.

### Key Advantage: No Income Limitation

Unlike Section 179, bonus depreciation has **no income limitation**. It can create a net operating loss (NOL) at the entity level that flows through to each surgeon-owner's personal return as a pass-through loss. Owners can carry that loss forward at 80% of future taxable income under current law — a meaningful safety valve for equipment-heavy investment years.

## Provision 2: Section 179 — Doubled to $2,560,000

### The New Parameters

| Parameter | Pre-OBBBA (2024) | OBBBA Base (2025) | 2026 Inflation-Adjusted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maximum deduction | ~$1,050,000 | $2,500,000 | **$2,560,000** |
| Phase-out begins | ~$2,620,000 | $4,000,000 | **$4,090,000** |
| Full phase-out | ~$3,670,000 | $6,500,000 | **$6,650,000** |
| Effective date | — | January 1, 2025 (full TY 2025) | Ongoing |

**Important: Section 179 is effective from January 1, 2025** — unlike bonus depreciation, which has the January 19 acquisition date threshold. Equipment placed in service January 1–19, 2025 can use the new $2.5M Section 179 limit even though it does not qualify for 100% bonus depreciation.

### The Income Limitation — Why It Matters

Section 179 cannot create or increase a net operating loss. The deduction is capped at the entity's (or individual's) taxable income from active business operations. If an ASC purchases $600,000 in equipment in a year when the entity only has $400,000 in taxable income, the Section 179 deduction is limited to $400,000 — with the remaining $200,000 carried forward to the next year.

**Strategic application:** Use Section 179 first (up to available taxable income), then stack bonus depreciation for any remaining basis. They are not mutually exclusive.

### Interaction with Pass-Through Entities

For surgeon-owned ASCs structured as LLCs or S-corps:
- The entity elects Section 179 at the entity level (Form 4562)
- Each surgeon-owner's allocable share flows through on Schedule K-1
- The income limitation is tested at the **individual level** for partnerships/LLCs — meaning a surgeon's W-2 and other business income counts toward the ceiling, not just the ASC income

A surgeon earning $1.2M from their surgical practice easily absorbs a $50,000 K-1 Section 179 allocation regardless of whether the ASC itself shows a loss.

## Provision 3: QBI Deduction (Section 199A) — Permanent at 23%

### What Changed

The OBBBA made the pass-through deduction permanent and **increased the rate from 20% to 23%** for tax years beginning after December 31, 2025.

| Parameter | Pre-OBBBA | OBBBA (TY 2026+) |
|---|---|---|
| Deduction rate | 20% | **23%** |
| Status | Expired 12/31/2025 | **Permanent** |
| SSTB phase-out range (MFJ) | $75,000 above threshold | **$150,000 above threshold** |
| SSTB phase-out range (Single) | $50,000 above threshold | **$75,000 above threshold** |
| Minimum deduction | None | **$400 for QBI ≥ $1,000** |
| Effective date | — | TY beginning after 12/31/2025 |

### The SSTB Problem for Physicians

Healthcare is explicitly classified as a **Specified Service Trade or Business (SSTB)** under Section 199A(d). Physician-owned ASCs providing clinical services generally fall within this classification. The deduction phases out and is eliminated entirely for high-income SSTB owners:

**2026 estimated SSTB phase-out thresholds (MFJ):**
- Full 23% deduction available below: ~$395,000 taxable income
- Phase-out range: $395,000–$545,000
- Zero deduction above: ~$545,000

Most actively practicing orthopedic surgeons exceed $545,000 in taxable income and receive **no QBI deduction** from their practice or ASC income. Do not overstate this provision for high earners.

### The Equipment-QBI Interaction: A Hidden Planning Lever

For surgeons sitting in the $395,000–$545,000 phase-out range, a large Section 179 or bonus depreciation deduction creates a meaningful secondary benefit:

**Example:** A surgeon with $490,000 in taxable income (in the phase-out zone) takes a $150,000 Section 179 deduction from an Arvis purchase, reducing taxable income to $340,000 — below the $395,000 threshold. Result: the surgeon now qualifies for the **full 23% QBI deduction** on remaining qualified business income.

If that surgeon has $200,000 of QBI below the threshold: the recovered 23% QBI deduction generates an additional **$46,000 deduction** (worth ~$17,020 at 37%). This stacks on top of the $55,500 savings from the Section 179 deduction itself.

For surgeons in this specific income band, the total tax impact of an equipment purchase significantly exceeds the face value of the depreciation deduction alone. This is a conversation worth having with a CPA.

## Provision 4: Estate & Gift Tax — $15M Exemption, Permanent

Effective January 1, 2026, the federal estate and gift tax exemption increased to **$15 million per individual ($30 million per married couple)** — permanently, with no sunset.

### Why This Matters for ASC Owners

The prior TCJA exemption (~$13.99M) was scheduled to sunset to approximately $7M on January 1, 2026. The OBBBA eliminated that cliff entirely and raised the floor.

**Direct applications:**
- A surgeon holding a 25% stake in an ASC valued at $20M (enterprise value) has a $5M ownership interest — which now transfers estate-tax-free below the $15M individual threshold
- Physician groups with multiple owners considering buy-sell structures, cross-purchase agreements, or succession plans can now model those transitions without federal estate tax exposure at most reasonable ASC valuations
- The permanent status eliminates the "use it or lose it" urgency that was driving rushed estate planning transactions before the old sunset

## Provision 5: Section 163(j) EBITDA Restoration — Debt-Financed Buyers

For facilities financing Arvis with borrowed capital, the OBBBA restored the business interest deduction limit to an **EBITDA-based calculation** (vs. EBIT, which had applied 2022–2024).

The practical difference: EBITDA adds back depreciation and amortization before calculating the interest deduction limit. For capital-intensive ASCs carrying acquisition debt or equipment financing:
- Under EBIT (old rule): Depreciation was excluded from the base → smaller interest deduction allowed
- Under EBITDA (restored): Depreciation adds back to the base → larger interest deduction allowed

For a highly leveraged ASC development structure, restoring EBITDA can meaningfully increase the deductible portion of debt service in early years when depreciation charges are highest.

## Provision 6: Section 179D — Hard Deadline June 30, 2026

The Section 179D energy-efficient commercial building deduction was **permanently terminated for construction beginning after June 30, 2026**.

For ASCs constructing or renovating facilities with qualifying improvements:
- **Deduction rate (with prevailing wage/apprenticeship requirements):** $2.50–$5.00 per square foot
- **Deduction rate (without PWA requirements):** $0.50–$1.00 per square foot
- **Typical deduction for a 10,000 sq. ft. ASC:** $25,000–$50,000 (with PWA)
- **The trigger:** Physical construction must begin (or a 5% safe harbor must be met) **before June 30, 2026** — completion date is irrelevant

This is not about Arvis directly — it is about the building that houses it. For any first-time developer or facility undergoing renovation, this deadline is a $25,000–$50,000 decision that cannot be recovered once missed.

## State Tax Conformity — The Caveat That Changes the Numbers

Several major states do not conform to federal bonus depreciation rules. In these states, bonus depreciation taken on the federal return must be added back on the state return, and the asset is depreciated under the state's own schedule.

| State | Conforms to OBBBA Bonus Dep.? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| California | **No** | CA decouples entirely; straight-line depreciation only |
| New York | **No** | NY does not conform; significant add-back |
| New Jersey | **No** | NJ historically decouples |
| Illinois | Partial | Conformity varies by provision |
| Texas | No state income tax | N/A — no state income tax |
| Florida | No state income tax | N/A — no state income tax |
| Most other states | **Yes** | Generally conform unless noted |

For a surgeon-owner in California, the **federal tax savings remain fully intact** — California income taxes are separate and the federal deduction is not affected by California's failure to conform. The difference is simply that the surgeon owes California income tax on the income that federal bonus depreciation sheltered, calculated at California rates. At California's top marginal rate of 13.3%, a $200,000 federal deduction that California does not recognize costs approximately $26,600 in additional California state tax. The federal benefit still dominates — but state CPA advice is essential for accurate after-tax cost modeling.

## The MACRS Baseline: What Happens Without Acceleration

*For facilities that do not elect Section 179 or bonus depreciation on a $300,000 system:*

Medical equipment falls under **5-year MACRS property** (Asset Class 57.0, Health Services) using the 200% declining balance method:

| Year | MACRS % | Deduction | Tax Savings (37%) | Discounted Value (8%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20.00% | $60,000 | $22,200 | $22,200 |
| 2 | 32.00% | $96,000 | $35,520 | $32,889 |
| 3 | 19.20% | $57,600 | $21,312 | $16,924 |
| 4 | 11.52% | $34,560 | $12,787 | $9,403 |
| 5 | 11.52% | $34,560 | $12,787 | $8,707 |
| 6 | 5.76% | $17,280 | $6,394 | $4,038 |
| **Total** | **100%** | **$300,000** | **$111,000** | **$94,161** |

**Nominal tax savings are identical** either way — $111,000 total over the asset's life. The entire advantage of Section 179 / bonus depreciation is **time value**: receiving $111,000 in Year 1 versus $94,161 in present value terms under MACRS represents a **$16,839 improvement in NPV** at an 8% discount rate.

That $16,839 is real money that the physician-owner can invest, pay down debt, or distribute — and it is the result of receiving the same tax savings 5 years earlier.
