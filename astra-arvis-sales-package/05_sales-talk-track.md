# Section 5: Sales Rep Talk Track & Objection Handling

> This guide is for internal sales agent use only. All financial figures cited are based on publicly available 2026 data and are for conversational context only — not financial advice. Always direct customers to their own CPA or financial advisor for specific guidance.

---

## Reading the Room: Who Is Sitting Across the Table?

Before opening your mouth, identify which of four buyer types you are talking to. Each has a different primary motivation and a different conversation entry point.

| Buyer | Primary Motivation | Open With |
|---|---|---|
| **For-profit hospital CFO/VP** | Capital allocation efficiency, margin | Tax savings + volume retention from ASC competition |
| **Non-profit hospital administrator** | Volume, quality, competitive positioning | Case migration risk + newly reimbursable procedure list |
| **Established ASC surgeon-owner** | After-tax cash return, equity value | "The tax system covers $74K of a $200K purchase in Year 1" |
| **First-time ASC developer** | Understanding the financial model | Break-even, financing, Day 1 capability |

---

## Talk Track 1: For-Profit Hospital (CFO or VP Finance)

**Opening:**
> "Before we talk about the system itself, I want to give you some context on the capital side. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed last July, permanently restored 100% first-year bonus depreciation on equipment like this. On a $300K purchase at a 21% corporate rate, that's $63,000 in immediate tax savings — cash that stays on your balance sheet instead of going to the IRS. Has your finance team looked at your equipment capital plan through that lens for 2026?"

**The volume retention angle:**
> "The other piece I'd put in front of your orthopedic leadership is what CMS just opened up. They removed 285 musculoskeletal procedures from the Inpatient-Only list effective January 2026. Revision joints and complex spine fusions are now billable outpatient. The facilities that are going to capture that volume are the ones with navigation capability. An HOPD doing TKA earns roughly $4,700 more per case than an ASC on Medicare — but only if you keep those cases in your facility. Every case that walks to a navigation-equipped competitor costs you that spread."

**Close:**
> "I'm not asking you to make a capital decision today. What I'd like to do is put together a one-page financial case for your committee showing the depreciation timing benefit, the volume retention value, and the GPO pricing we can access — typically 10–20% off list through Vizient or Premier. Can we schedule 30 minutes with your OR director in the room?"

## Talk Track 2: Non-Profit Hospital (Administrator or CMO)

**Opening:**
> "I want to talk about what CMS's 2026 rules mean for your orthopedic program specifically, because the competitive landscape just shifted. Two hundred and eighty-five musculoskeletal procedures that were inpatient-only 12 months ago are now outpatient-eligible. Revision total hip and knee are on that list. The facilities that can perform those cases outpatient are going to capture that volume — and the facilities that can't are going to watch it walk to ASCs."

**Quality and outcomes angle:**
> "The clinical evidence is straightforward. Computer-assisted navigation reduces implant malalignment outliers from roughly 32% to 9% in TKA. For your program, that's fewer revisions, better outcomes data, and stronger quality metrics for commercial contract renewals. In a DRG environment, every avoided revision is direct margin improvement."

**Addressing the 'no tax benefit' objection:**
> "I understand the depreciation angle doesn't apply the same way here — you're tax-exempt. But the financial case isn't a tax story for you. It's a volume story. With 51% of total joints projected to move outpatient by 2026, the question isn't whether this volume migrates — it's whether it migrates to your outpatient program or to a competitor's ASC. Navigation is the clinical capability that makes complex outpatient orthopedics possible."

## Talk Track 3: Established Surgeon-Owner (ASC Partner)

**Opening:**
> "I want to show you something quickly before we talk about the system. At your tax bracket — let's assume 37% federal — a $200,000 equipment purchase this year has a real after-tax cost of $126,000. Bonus depreciation is permanent now under the new law. The IRS covers $74,000 of the purchase price in Year 1. That's not a lease, that's not a payment plan — that's $74,000 that hits your personal tax return as a deduction the same year you install the system."

**The equity angle:**
> "The other dimension that I think gets underweighted in these conversations is what this does to your ownership value. If this system enables 50 additional high-value cases per year at your center, that's roughly $225,000 in incremental EBITDA at standard orthopedic ASC margins. At a 9x multiple — which is conservative for an orthopedic-focused center — that's $2 million in enterprise value. Your percentage of that is your percentage of the center."

**On the new procedure list:**
> "And I want to flag something specific to 2026: revision total hip and knee are now on the ASC covered procedures list. Those are your highest-facility-fee cases. Without navigation, your center can't safely perform them. With it, you're in a different tier of ASC entirely."

**Handling 'we'll budget for it next year':**
> "Here's the math on waiting: the depreciation benefit is permanent — it doesn't expire. But every month you wait is a month those cases go somewhere else. If your center does 4 additional TJAs per month at $10,000 blended average, that's $480,000 in revenue over the 12 months you spent deciding. And you've deferred the tax deduction by a full year, which at 8% cost of capital is about $5,900 in lost present value on the depreciation alone. The financial case gets stronger the sooner you act, not weaker."

## Talk Track 4: First-Time ASC Developer

**Opening:**
> "The financial model for a de novo orthopedic ASC has never been stronger than it is right now. CMS just opened up 560 additional procedure categories for ASC billing in 2026. Orthopedics generates the highest average net revenue per case of any ASC specialty — $6,419 on average, and significantly higher for total joints. A two-OR center breaks even at roughly 65–80 cases per month, which is below standard utilization for a committed orthopedic group."

**On equipment timing:**
> "One thing I want to be direct about: don't phase your equipment purchase. I know the instinct when you're managing a $6M startup budget is to defer optional line items. But navigation and imaging aren't optional for orthopedic surgery — they're what allows you to perform the cases that generate your highest facility fees. And with 100% bonus depreciation being permanent now, you want to take that deduction in your first operating year, when your startup costs are highest and you need every tax offset available."

**On financing:**
> "For the equipment specifically, make sure you're financing with a loan, not a lease. A true lease means the lender owns the equipment — you don't get the depreciation deduction. At the 37% bracket, that's $111,000 in Year 1 tax savings you're leaving on the table. Healthcare equipment lenders are doing 7–11% on equipment loans for qualified physician groups — the after-tax cost of that interest is small relative to the depreciation benefit you capture."

**Handling 'I need to talk to my CPA first':**
> "Absolutely — and you should. What I'd ask is that when you do, make sure they're looking at the OBBBA provisions specifically. The Section 179 limit was doubled to $2.56 million and bonus depreciation was permanently restored at 100% for property purchased after January 19, 2025. If your CPA isn't familiar with those changes, it's worth a second conversation with a healthcare-specific tax advisor. The numbers are material enough that it changes the build-or-wait decision for most groups."

## The Four Most Common Objections — and How to Handle Them

### "We're in budget planning for next year."
> "Completely understandable. Let me ask — when does your capital budget cycle close? If there's a mid-year reallocation process, this purchase fits the authorization profile for CFO-level approval — you typically don't need full board sign-off at this price point. And if you're on a calendar year, placing this in service before December 31st means the tax deduction hits 2026. January 2nd is a different tax year."

### "We don't know if the ROI is there."
> "The ROI question really comes down to one variable: how many cases per month do your surgeons commit to bringing to this system? If you have three orthopedic partners who currently take their total joints to a hospital — and those surgeons are telling you they'd prefer to work at your ASC — the math is straightforward. Four to five TJAs per month at $10,000 blended revenue generates $480,000–$600,000 in annual revenue. That's payback in under 12 months before the tax deduction. Can we put together a utilization model based on your specific group?"

### "We're looking at [competitor system]."
> "That's worth a real comparison. What I'd ask you to evaluate side by side is total cost of ownership — not just list price, but service contract terms, upgrade cycles, and the procedure categories each system supports. Arvis was designed specifically for orthopedic navigation workflows, and for ASC case pace and room turnover requirements. I'd like to bring our clinical specialist in to walk through a workflow comparison specific to your case mix."

### "We're not sure about navigation for our patient population."
> "The clinical evidence is fairly clear at this point. Computer-assisted navigation reduces mechanical axis malalignment outliers from 32% to 9% in TKA — that's not a marketing claim, that's peer-reviewed meta-analysis data. For revision procedures — which are now ASC-eligible for the first time in 2026 — navigation isn't optional, it's a clinical standard. The question isn't whether navigation produces better outcomes; the question is whether your facility will be equipped to offer it."

## Closing Framework

**For any buyer type, close on a specific next step — never leave without one:**

1. **30-minute financial modeling session** with the CFO/administrator and your clinical specialist
2. **Site visit** to a reference ASC currently using Arvis (peer-to-peer surgeon conversations close more than any sales rep presentation)
3. **Utilization analysis** built on their specific surgeon commitments and case mix
4. **GPO quote** through their Vizient/Premier membership to anchor the real purchase price

> *"I'm not here to sell you a piece of equipment today. I want to be the person your team thinks of as a resource when you're building the financial case for this investment. Let me do the work of putting together a custom analysis — you bring it to your committee, and if it doesn't hold up, we part as friends. Fair?"*
