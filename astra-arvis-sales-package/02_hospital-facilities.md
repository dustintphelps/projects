## The Hospital Decision Landscape

Hospitals operate in two fundamentally different financial environments depending on tax status — and the Arvis case is built differently for each.

---

## For-Profit Hospitals: The Tax Play Is Real

A for-profit hospital purchasing Astra Arvis in 2026 gets the same accelerated depreciation benefit as any physician-owned entity.

**The math on a $300K system:**
- 100% bonus depreciation (permanent under OBBBA) → **$300,000 deduction in Year 1**
- At 21% corporate rate: **$63,000 in immediate tax savings**
- Net after-tax cost: **$237,000**

Contrast that with old-law 20% bonus depreciation (what 2026 would have been without OBBBA):
- Year 1 deduction: $60,000 → only $12,600 in Year 1 tax savings
- Remaining $240,000 deducted over 5 years

**OBBBA restored $50,400 in Year 1 cash benefit** that old law would have deferred to future years. That is real capital that stays on the balance sheet rather than going to the IRS.

**Financing note:** Equipment financed with tax-exempt bonds must use Alternative Depreciation System (ADS) — no bonus depreciation available. For-profit hospitals using conventional bank debt or internal funds get the full benefit.

## Non-Profit Hospitals: The Operational ROI Case

Non-profit hospital systems pay no federal income tax. The depreciation analysis is irrelevant to their financial position. The case for Arvis at a non-profit is built entirely on three operational levers:

### Lever 1: Volume Retention — Stop the Bleed to ASCs

**CMS's 2026 rule removed 285 musculoskeletal procedures from the Inpatient-Only list** — meaning complex orthopedic cases that were exclusively billable as hospital admissions are now performable in outpatient and ASC settings.

The HOPD Medicare facility fee for a Total Knee Arthroplasty is approximately **$14,005** (2026 estimated). The ASC Medicare rate for the same procedure is approximately **$9,284**. Hospitals earn approximately **$4,700 more per TKA** in the HOPD setting than an ASC does.

Every TKA that migrates from your HOPD to a competitor's navigation-equipped ASC costs the hospital approximately **$4,700 in Medicare facility fee alone** — and considerably more in commercial cases where HOPD facility fees can reach $25,000–$35,000.

With **51% of total joints projected to shift to outpatient settings by 2026**, a hospital that doesn't offer navigation-capable outpatient orthopedics is building a leaky bucket. Navigation capability is increasingly the differentiator that keeps high-volume orthopedic surgeons — and their cases — in the hospital system.

### Lever 2: New Revenue from Newly Eligible Cases

The new **Level 7 Musculoskeletal APC** created in the CY 2026 OPPS Final Rule opens billing for **revision total hip arthroplasty, revision total knee arthroplasty, and complex multilevel spinal fusions** that were previously inpatient-only — procedures that require imaging and navigation to perform safely in an outpatient environment.

These are the highest-value procedures in orthopedics. A hospital with navigation infrastructure can now capture outpatient billing for cases it was previously forced to admit.

### Lever 3: OR Efficiency and Cost-Per-Case

Navigation reduces implant malalignment outliers from **31.8% to 9.0%** (conventional vs. computer-assisted TKA). Fewer malalignment events means fewer early revisions, lower readmission costs, and stronger quality metrics that support commercial contract renewals.

In an inpatient DRG environment where the hospital absorbs revision costs within a fixed payment, a navigated surgical program directly reduces cost-per-case.

## The Capital Budget Argument

For hospitals with **calendar-year fiscal years**, the Q2–Q3 2025 capital budget cycle has passed — but most large systems maintain a mid-year capital reallocation process for revenue-generating items. A $100K–$500K system falls within **CFO or VP authorization limits** at most health systems; board approval is generally not required, enabling faster decision cycles than robotic platform purchases.

**For hospitals on an October fiscal year:** Placing Arvis in service by September 30, 2026 captures a full fiscal year of benefit.

---

## GPO Leverage — Use It

Vizient and Premier group-buy programs for imaging and navigation capital equipment have delivered **10–27% savings off list price** in documented group purchasing events. A hospital that is a GPO member should formally query the capital equipment contract catalog before finalizing purchase terms. On a $300K system, a 15% GPO discount is $45,000 in direct acquisition savings.

---

## The Spine Navigation Exception — Separately Billable

For hospitals with spine surgery programs: unlike extremity navigation CPTs (which are bundled under comprehensive APCs and not separately reimbursed by Medicare), **spine navigation CPT codes 61781–61783 are separately billable** under OPPS and carry distinct APC payments in complex deformity and revision spine cases. This creates a direct reimbursement premium for spine navigation that does not exist for joint replacement navigation — a meaningful differentiator when building the spine program financial case.

---

## Summary: The Hospital Case in Three Sentences

A for-profit hospital saves $63,000 in cash taxes in Year 1 on a $300K Arvis system while protecting against case migration that costs $4,700+ per TKA in lost facility fee revenue. A non-profit hospital cannot use the tax benefit but can access newly reimbursable high-acuity outpatient cases opened by the 2026 CMS rule changes, retain high-value surgeons, and reduce revision-related cost. Both hospital types face the same competitive reality: navigation-equipped ASCs and competing hospitals are already capturing this volume.
