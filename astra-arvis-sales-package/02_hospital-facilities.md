## Two Very Different Financial Conversations

Hospital facilities require completely different financial frameworks depending on tax status. The mistake many sales reps make is leading with the tax story for a non-profit system where the CFO will correctly point out that depreciation is irrelevant to their tax position. Know your audience before you walk in.

---

## For-Profit Hospitals: The Depreciation Story Is Real and Material

### Year 1 Tax Impact

Under the OBBBA's permanent 100% bonus depreciation, a for-profit hospital purchasing a $250,000 Arvis system generates a **$250,000 federal tax deduction in the purchase year** at the 21% corporate rate — producing **$52,500 in immediate cash tax savings**.

Compare that to how 2026 would have looked without OBBBA (20% bonus depreciation year):
- Old law Year 1 deduction: $50,000 (20% of $250,000)
- Old law Year 1 tax savings: **$10,500**
- **OBBBA restored $42,000 in Year 1 cash** on a $250,000 purchase

The remaining value under MACRS would have accrued over years 2–6 in present value terms. The full MACRS NPV at 8% discount: approximately $52,400 — essentially identical in total but delivered over five fewer years under bonus depreciation. For a hospital system managing capital allocation quarterly, Year 1 cash flow matters.

### GAAP vs. Tax: Understanding the Two Books

For-profit hospitals maintain two depreciation schedules. This is important because finance teams and bond analysts care about **book (GAAP)** depreciation, while the tax benefit flows from **IRS** depreciation:

| | GAAP (Book) | IRS (Tax) |
|---|---|---|
| Method | Straight-line | 100% Bonus Depreciation |
| Useful life (nav/imaging) | 5–7 years (per AHA guide) | Year 1 (immediate) |
| Year 1 on $250K system | ~$35,700–$50,000 | $250,000 |
| Creates deferred tax? | — | Yes — ~$45,000 DTL |
| Impact on EBITDA | No impact (adds to D&A) | Reduces taxable income |

The $45,000 deferred tax liability created by accelerated tax depreciation unwinds over the GAAP useful life — it is a timing difference only, not a permanent difference. The total tax savings over the asset's life are identical under either method; bonus depreciation simply accelerates when that cash is received.

**Implication for bond-covenant conversations:** GAAP depreciation (not tax depreciation) drives the income statement and EBITDA reported to bond rating agencies. A system concerned about EBITDA covenant compliance should note that accelerating tax depreciation does not reduce GAAP EBITDA. The two are fully independent.

### Equipment Financing: Preserve the Full Benefit

The bonus depreciation benefit applies when the hospital **owns** the equipment. Tax-exempt bond financing introduces a critical exception: **property financed with tax-exempt bonds must use the Alternative Depreciation System (ADS)** — straight-line over longer recovery periods, with no bonus depreciation available.

For-profit hospitals financing Arvis through:
- **Conventional bank debt or internal funds:** Full bonus depreciation available
- **Tax-exempt bond proceeds (Section 145):** ADS required — no acceleration
- **Operating lease:** Hospital does not own the equipment, no depreciation at all

For a for-profit hospital with access to both taxable and tax-exempt financing, the after-tax cost of the depreciation benefit should be explicitly modeled before choosing the financing source.

## Non-Profit Hospitals: Build the Operational Case

A non-profit hospital system pays no federal income tax. The depreciation analysis is a non-conversation — do not lead with it or spend time on it. The financial case for a non-profit is built entirely on three operational pillars.

### Pillar 1: Revenue Retention from a Shifting Procedure Landscape

**The CMS 2026 structural change:** The CY 2026 OPPS/ASC Final Rule removed **285 musculoskeletal procedures from the Inpatient-Only list** and created a new **Level 7 Musculoskeletal APC** to accommodate complex joint revisions and multilevel fusions that now qualify for outpatient billing. These are the highest-acuity, highest-facility-fee orthopedic procedures. They now can be performed — and billed — in ASC settings for the first time.

**The revenue differential at stake:**

| Procedure | HOPD Medicare Fee (est. CY 2026) | ASC Medicare Fee (est. CY 2026) | HOPD Premium Per Case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total Knee Arthroplasty (27447) | ~$14,005 | ~$9,284 | **~$4,721** |
| Total Hip Arthroplasty (27130) | ~$14,005 | ~$9,478 | **~$4,527** |
| Knee arthroscopy w/ meniscus repair (29882) | ~$2,170 | ~$1,050 | **~$1,120** |
| Knee arthroscopy, limited (29870–76) | ~$1,400 | ~$700 | **~$700** |

*On-campus HOPDs retain full OPPS rates. Off-campus HOPDs acquired after November 2, 2015 are subject to site-neutral payment at approximately 40% of OPPS — similar to ASC rates. Confirm your HOPD's campus designation before modeling revenue.*

**Commercial payer context:** For commercial cases (often 40–60% of orthopedic procedure revenue), HOPD facility fees range from **150% to 300%+ of Medicare rates** depending on contract vintage and market. The per-case revenue differential versus an ASC can reach **$10,000–$20,000 per TJA** in major markets. Every TKA or THA that migrates from a hospital HOPD to a navigation-equipped ASC represents a significant permanent revenue loss in commercial payer revenue — not just the CMS differential.

**The migration thesis:** With 51% of total joints projected to shift to outpatient settings by 2026 (Sg2 Health Consultants), and ASC joint replacement volume growing at a 6.7% annual CAGR, hospitals that are not equipped to perform navigation-dependent complex cases in their outpatient programs are building a structural revenue decline into their orthopedic service lines. The procedures are moving. The question is whether they move within the hospital system or to competitors.

### Pillar 2: New Revenue from Newly Eligible Outpatient Cases

The 2026 rule created billable procedures that did not previously exist as HOPD outpatient cases. **Revision total hip arthroplasty, revision total knee arthroplasty, and complex multilevel spinal fusions** — previously inpatient-only — are now assignable to outpatient APCs including the new Level 7 Musculoskeletal APC.

These procedures:
- Carry higher facility fees than primary arthroplasty (revision complexity)
- Were historically only billable under DRG 469/470 as inpatient cases (see below)
- Require navigation and imaging to safely execute in an ambulatory environment
- Create immediate new revenue categories for HOPD programs with navigation capability

A hospital system that installs navigation in its outpatient orthopedic program in 2026 can bill for procedure categories that were literally not billable as outpatient cases 12 months ago.

### Pillar 3: Cost-Per-Case Reduction in a Bundled Payment World

**Inpatient DRG context:** For patients who do require inpatient admission for joint replacement, the CY 2026 IPPS assigned approximately:
- DRG 470 (TKA/THA without MCC): **~$14,200–$15,000** hospital facility payment
- DRG 469 (TKA/THA with MCC): **~$18,000–$22,000**

These are all-inclusive payments. Revision surgery, extended stays, or complications are absorbed by the hospital within the DRG. Navigation reduces the revision risk profile of the original procedure.

**The revision cost argument:** A navigated TKA program that reduces malalignment outliers from 31.8% to 9.0% (per peer-reviewed meta-analysis) does not generate additional Medicare billing — but it generates substantial internal cost savings. Early revisions triggered by malalignment drive additional DRG admissions, surgical team costs, implant costs, and liability exposure. Each avoided revision at the DRG 469/470 payment level represents $14,000–$22,000 in hospital costs that are NOT reimbursed by a second bundled payment. Navigation turns revision avoidance into direct operating margin improvement.

**The spine billing exception:** Navigation CPT codes for orthopedic extremity procedures (0054T, 0055T) are **bundled under comprehensive APCs** and not separately reimbursed by Medicare in the HOPD setting. However, **spine navigation CPT codes 61781–61783 ARE separately billable** as add-on codes to spine fusion procedures under OPPS. For hospital systems with spine surgery programs, navigation capability generates both the quality outcomes benefit AND a direct incremental billing item — a fundamentally different ROI calculation than for joint replacement.

## Capital Budget Mechanics: How Hospitals Actually Buy

### Budget Cycle and Authorization Thresholds

Hospital capital budgeting typically follows this timeline:
- **Q2 of prior year:** Department-level requests submitted with ROI justifications
- **Q3 of prior year:** Capital committee prioritization; CFO review
- **Q4 of prior year / Q1 of budget year:** Executive or board approval; POs issued

For a $100,000–$500,000 imaging or navigation system, most large health systems fall within **CFO or VP-level authorization** — board approval is typically required only for purchases above $1M–$5M depending on system size. This is a critical deal-velocity insight: Arvis can be approved and ordered faster than robotic surgery platforms, which routinely require full board cycles.

**Mid-year capital reallocation:** Most systems maintain a mechanism for urgent unbudgeted capital items — particularly revenue-generating investments. A navigation system positioned as a **volume retention tool** (framed as preventing case migration to ASC competitors) often qualifies as a revenue-protection investment that finance committees approve out-of-cycle.

### GPO Pricing: Don't Leave 10–27% on the Table

The three largest healthcare GPOs — **Vizient** (largest by hospital membership), **Premier**, and **HealthTrust Performance Group** — all maintain capital equipment contract catalogs including imaging and orthopedic navigation systems. Key facts for hospital sales conversations:

- GPO contracts for capital equipment function as **framework agreements** — negotiated price floors or discount schedules, not fixed unit prices
- Vizient and Premier both run **group buy programs** for capital equipment that have delivered documented savings of **10–27% off list price** in participating events
- A hospital GPO member should formally query their group purchasing portal for any existing contract terms before finalizing a direct purchase
- On a $300,000 system, a 15% GPO discount is **$45,000 in direct acquisition savings** — material enough to change a budget committee decision

**Implant bundling consideration:** For navigation systems integrated with implant ecosystems, the capital equipment contract and the implant contract are linked. GPO implant volume commitments and tiered pricing affect total cost of ownership significantly. Ensure the finance team is modeling total system economics, not just equipment list price.

## Summary: The Non-Profit Hospital Case in Numbers

A 200-bed community hospital orthopedic program performing 300 TKAs annually:

| Scenario | Revenue per TKA (Medicare) | Revenue per TKA (Commercial, avg.) | Annual Revenue (50% Medicare, 50% Commercial) |
|---|---|---|---|
| All cases remain HOPD | $14,005 | $28,000 est. | **$6.3M** |
| 20% migrate to ASC (60 cases) | — | — | **$5.6M (-$700K)** |
| 35% migrate to ASC (105 cases) | — | — | **$4.97M (-$1.33M)** |

The break-even question for a non-profit CFO: **how many cases per year can afford to migrate before the navigation system investment pays for itself?** At a $5,000 blended revenue differential per case, a $250,000 navigation system is covered by retaining 50 cases. A 200-case-per-year orthopedic program retaining even 25% of its at-risk volume recovers the investment in under 6 months.
