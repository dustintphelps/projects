# Section 3: Surgeon-Owned ASCs — Tax, Distributions & Equity Value

All figures are illustrative estimates. Consult a qualified CPA, healthcare attorney, or financial advisor before making any investment decision based on this information.

---

## Why ASC Ownership Changes the Entire Equation

When a surgeon owns equity in the facility where they operate, a capital equipment purchase does three things simultaneously:

1. **Generates an immediate personal tax deduction** (the depreciation flows through to their individual return)
2. **Enables revenue from day one** (the equipment unlocks procedure capability that produces facility fee distributions)
3. **Increases the equity value of their ownership stake** (additional EBITDA × the ASC valuation multiple)

No other investment structure produces that three-way simultaneity. Understanding all three dimensions is what separates a transactional equipment sale from a genuine capital planning conversation.

---

## The Tax Flow: How Equipment Purchases Reach a Surgeon's Personal Return

Most surgeon-owned ASCs are structured as **multi-member LLCs taxed as partnerships or S-corporations**. This means the ASC entity does not pay income tax — all income, deductions, and credits flow through to each surgeon-owner's personal Form 1040 via Schedule K-1.

**How a $200,000 Arvis system flows through a 25% ownership stake:**

1. The ASC elects 100% bonus depreciation on the $200,000 equipment purchase
2. Each surgeon-owner's K-1 reflects their pro-rata share of the deduction
3. A 25% owner receives a **$50,000 deduction** on their personal return
4. At a 37% marginal rate: **$18,500 in personal tax savings**
5. On the full $200,000 (100% ownership equivalent): **$74,000 in tax savings**

**The key advantage over Section 179:** Bonus depreciation has no income cap — it can create a pass-through loss even if the ASC itself shows a loss in a given year. Section 179 is capped at the entity's taxable income.

## Distributions: How the Cash Flows Back

ASC profits are distributed to physician-owners **quarterly, strictly pro-rata to ownership percentage** (required for Stark Law safe harbor compliance). The entity also typically makes **tax distributions** each quarter — cash distributions sized to cover each owner's estimated personal tax liability on their allocated income, so surgeons are not left paying taxes out of pocket on phantom income.

The distribution waterfall in plain terms: after the ASC covers operating reserves and debt service, remaining EBITDA distributes to owners in proportion to their equity stake. A 25% owner of a center generating $2M in annual EBITDA receives approximately **$500,000/year in distributions** before personal income tax.

## The Valuation Multiplier: Why Equipment Is Worth More Than Its Sticker Price

Every dollar of EBITDA permanently added through owned (rather than rented) equipment or new procedure capability is multiplied at exit. EBITDA multiples for orthopedic-focused ASCs in 2025–2026:

- Single-specialty physician-owned: **5–8x**
- Orthopedic-focused: **7–10x**
- Regional/platform ASCs: **11–17x**

An ASC that currently rents a C-arm at $20,000/year and purchases instead eliminates $20,000 in annual expense → $20,000 in EBITDA → **$140,000–$200,000 in enterprise value** at 7–10x. That is the valuation leverage on equipment ownership.

## The QBI Opportunity (Income-Specific)

For surgeon-owners with taxable income **below approximately $545,000 (MFJ)**, the OBBBA's permanent 23% QBI deduction may apply to ASC pass-through income. A large Section 179 deduction that pulls taxable income below the phase-out threshold can recover this deduction — generating an additional $14,000–$18,000 in tax savings beyond the depreciation benefit itself.

For surgeons well above the threshold: QBI is unavailable, but the bonus depreciation benefit is identical regardless of income level.

---

## Estate Planning Upside — New in 2026

The OBBBA raised the estate and gift tax exemption to **$15M per individual ($30M per couple)**, effective January 1, 2026, with no sunset. ASC ownership interests that previously created estate tax exposure can now be held and transferred without federal estate tax for most physician-owner estates. This is particularly relevant for surgeons with large ASC equity positions considering succession planning or partner buyout structures.

## The Case in One Paragraph

A surgeon with a 25% ownership stake in an orthopedic ASC who purchases a $200,000 Arvis navigation system in 2026 receives a $50,000 personal tax deduction worth $18,500 in immediate cash savings, reducing the real cost of their $50,000 capital contribution to $31,500. If the system enables 50 additional high-value cases per year at industry-average contribution margins, it generates approximately $225,000 in incremental annual EBITDA — of which the surgeon's 25% distributable share is $56,250/year, while the EBITDA multiplier adds $394,000–$563,000 to the surgeon's equity value at exit. The effective first-year return on a $31,500 after-tax investment, combining distributions and equity appreciation, is multiples of the initial outlay.
