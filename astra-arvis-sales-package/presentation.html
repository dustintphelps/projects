Objection Handling

The Four Objections Every Rep Encounters

"It's not in this year's capital budget."
Most $100–500K revenue-generating purchases fall within CFO or VP-level authorization — no full board cycle needed. Frame as revenue retention. A $5,000 blended differential per case means a $250K system is covered by retaining 50 cases. Ask: "What's your authorization threshold for unbudgeted revenue-protection items?"
"We need to see better ROI data."
One variable dominates: how many cases per month does your committed surgeon group bring? 4 additional TJAs/month = $480K/year revenue. At $126K after-tax cost, payback is under 4 months. Ask to spend 30 minutes building a utilization model with their specific case volume and payer mix.
"We're evaluating [Competitor X]."
A comparison worth doing thoroughly. Evaluate: total cost of ownership including service contract and upgrade obligations; specific procedure categories enabled vs. your case mix; OR setup time at steady state after the learning curve. Offer a peer-to-peer surgeon reference call — more useful than any demo.
"We want to wait on reimbursement landscape."
No payer pays a facility premium for navigation-assisted TJA — extremity codes (0054T/0055T) are bundled. No one should be selling you on a billing premium that doesn't exist. The case is volume (navigation enables the highest-fee categories), quality (outcomes data drives payer contracting leverage), and competitive position (20.4% of TKAs already robotic-assisted).
